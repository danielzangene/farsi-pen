How Farsi Pen should be built from here

The learning side of Farsi Pen is already deep. What it lacks is the platform around it: accounts, offline mode, payments and a way to ship reviewed content. This page proposes one architecture and shows each part of it as a simple diagram.

Recommendation: a local-first app with a thin backend. Keep everything that works today running on the learner's device, and add a small server only for the things a device can't do alone.

  1. The device stays in charge of learning. Lessons, reviews, scoring and audio work offline, as most of them already do.
  2. The server keeps a copy, not the original. The device sends a log of what the learner did; the server stores it for sync across devices, streak checks and leaderboards.
  3. Paid features are the ones that cost money to run. An AI conversation partner and accurate server-side speech scoring go in Pro. Lessons and offline mode stay free.
  4. Content goes through review before it ships. A pipeline moves each batch from draft to native-speaker approval to published, and generates its audio automatically.

1 · Who else teaches Persian

The market has a gap where Duolingo isn't

Duolingo still doesn't teach Persian. The closest products are a learnfarsi.app-style course app, a community-led podcast course, and generic multi-language apps where Persian is one of many. None of them combines writing, real conversation practice and spoken Tehrani Persian the way Farsi Pen already does.

Who the users are

  • Heritage learners who understand some spoken Persian but can't read or write it. The Iranian diaspora is large in the US (roughly 385,000–577,000 by Census-based counts), Canada and Germany.
  • Partners and family of Persian speakers, who need everyday spoken phrases and ta'ârof.
  • Travellers, who need the missions (taxi, café, bazaar).

What that means for the design

  • Teach spoken Tehrani first, with formal forms shown. Competitors mix them up.
  • Reading and writing is the heritage learner's real gap, so the alphabet and tracing matter more here than in a generic app.
  • Language apps convert few free users to paid (often quoted at 2–5%), so the free tier has to be good and the paid tier has to be something people clearly value.

2 · Three ways to build it

Option B fits a small team and the product you already have

Where dev is now

A · Static app only

Everything lives in the browser. No server at all.

  • Costs almost nothing to host
  • Clearing the browser loses all progress
  • No sync, no leaderboards, no payments
  • No way to stop someone faking XP
Recommended

B · Local-first app + thin backend

The device does the learning; a small server keeps a synced copy and handles accounts, payments and paid AI features.

  • Works offline, fast on slow networks
  • Keeps all of today's code; adds to it
  • Server costs grow only with paid features
  • Matches the spec's chosen stack (Supabase, Stripe)
Too much for now

C · Server builds every lesson

Like Duolingo: a model on the server scores about 200 candidate exercises and a session service picks each lesson.

  • Best personalisation at huge scale
  • Needs a network for every lesson
  • Needs a data team and lots of learners to train on
  • Rewrites the engine that works today

4 · Design layer

The app on the device

PWA · offline-first
ScreenLearner taps "Check"
→
Learning engineGrades answer, updates card
→
Local databaseSaves new state and an event
→
OutboxEvent waits until online
→
SyncSent in a batch later

What changes

  • Move progress from localStorage to IndexedDB. It holds more and suits an outbox.
  • Add a service worker so the whole app opens offline. The audio is already local, which makes this cheap.
  • Download content packs per level, so a phone doesn't fetch all 72 MB at once.

Why

  • Learners practise on the train, abroad and on weak connections.
  • Every action is instant because nothing waits for a server.
  • learnfarsi.app charges for offline mode; giving it free is a selling point.

5 · Design layer

One learning engine for everything

items → cards → sessions
ItemsWord, phrase, letter, grammar point, mission skill, sound
→
CardsOne memory state per item, spaced repetition
→
Session builderDue reviews + one new thing + weakest sound
→
ExerciseChoose, type, say, trace, role-play
→
Review eventResult written back to the card

What changes

  • Today there are four ways to review (Today plan, Daily Practice, the Words deck, My cards). Make them one queue that every item type feeds.
  • One session builder decides what comes next, the way Duolingo's session generator does, but on the device and with simple rules.

Why

  • Learners stop wondering which review button to press.
  • Letters, grammar and pronunciation weaknesses come back for review, not only words.
  • The review log is the data a smarter model would need later, if Farsi Pen ever grows enough for Option C.

6 · Design layer

Content pipeline with native review

draft → reviewed → published
DraftBatch written as structured files
→
Native reviewReviewer approves each row; status saved
→
ChecksExisting tests: every word, lesson and mission valid
→
AudioGenerated for new text; better voices or real recordings later
→
PackVersioned and published to the CDN

What changes

  • Each item gets a review status. Only approved items ship to the live site; drafts can stay on dev.
  • The reviewer approves in a shared sheet or as a required pull-request reviewer.
  • Audio stays on the open Piper voice for now. Azure has Persian neural voices (Dilara, Farid) for an upgrade, and the 500 most common words could get real human recordings.

Why

  • The spec requires native approval, and the "drafted for review" label was removed on dev.
  • Quality is the promised edge over learnfarsi.app (no duplicates, one transliteration style).
  • Reports from learners land in the same queue instead of public GitHub issues.

7 · Design layer

Accounts and sync as an event log

append-only · conflict-free
GuestLearns with no account, as today
→
Sign inEmail link or Google
→
Upload logGuest history is merged once
→
Server checksRecomputes XP and streak from events
→
Other devicesDownload the log and rebuild progress

What changes

  • The device sends what happened ("reviewed word 41, grade Good, 10:02"), never the final totals.
  • Events never change, so two phones can't conflict; the server just adds both.
  • Supabase covers accounts and the database, as the spec planned. A sync library such as PowerSync is optional later; check its web support first.

Why

  • Today, clearing the browser wipes progress. Manual backup files are a stopgap.
  • Leaderboards need XP the server has checked, or people will fake it.
  • Push reminders need an account to attach to.

8 · Design layer

Speech and AI in three tiers

free on device · paid on server
Tier 1 · FreeOn-device recognition and per-sound scoring, as on dev today
→
Tier 2 · ProServer speech recognition for more accurate scoring
→
Tier 3 · ProAI conversation partner for missions and scenes
→
AI gatewayKeeps API keys secret, limits minutes per user

What to know

  • Azure supports Persian speech-to-text and voices, but its pronunciation assessment doesn't appear to cover Persian. Keep Farsi Pen's own letter-level scoring.
  • Persian quality varies a lot between speech vendors. Test two or three with native speakers before choosing.
  • Chrome's built-in recognition sends audio to Google. That needs a clear privacy notice.

Why tiers

  • Server speech and AI cost money per minute; the free tier must not.
  • An AI partner that plays the taxi driver or Sara's aunt builds directly on the missions and scenes you already have.

9 · Design layer

Money: free core, Pro for what costs to run

one entitlement flag
FreeAll lessons, alphabet, grammar, missions, offline, on-device speech
→
ProAI conversation partner, server scoring, family sharing
→
StripeCheckout and customer portal
→
EntitlementOne "is Pro" flag the app checks

Suggested shape

  • Price near the market: learnfarsi.app charges $5.99/mo or $49.99/yr; Praktika about $8/mo.
  • A family plan suits heritage families where parents and children learn together.
  • Keep donations and a supporters wall from the spec for people who just want to help.

Why not lock lessons

  • Few free users ever pay in this category, so lessons are how people find and trust the app.
  • Charging for AI minutes keeps revenue in line with the only real running cost.
  • This answers the spec's open question (§4.3): Option A at launch, with Pro switched on when the AI tier is ready.

Grouping them by what the learner wants to do (learn, speak, review) puts every feature one tap from its tab. The فارسی sound and speaking tools also stop competing with each other for attention.

11 · Order of work

Four phases, each shippable on its own

1

Ready for a public launch

  • Privacy and terms pages (microphone use), and confirm the Yekan Bakh web licence
  • Service worker for offline use; move progress to IndexedDB
  • One review queue and the five-tab navigation
  • Native review pass and review status on content; analytics and error reports
2

Accounts and sync

  • Email-link and Google sign-in, guest merge, event-log sync
  • Push streak reminders (on iPhone only after "Add to Home Screen")
  • End-to-end tests for lessons, missions and sync
3

Pro

  • Stripe and the Pro flag; donations page
  • AI gateway, then the conversation partner in missions and scenes
  • Server speech scoring after a vendor test with native speakers
4

Light community

  • Leaderboards and friends from server-checked XP
  • Family plan and shared progress
  • Skip an open discussion board at first; it needs moderation the team doesn't have yet
